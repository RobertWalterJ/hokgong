# Accuracy audit — 20 September 2026

Run it yourself: `node audits/run-accuracy.mjs`, and `node audits/run-accuracy.mjs --sample 40`
for a sample to read by eye.

`build/verify.mjs` already proves the deck matches its sources — 101,869 checks
at every build. This is the other question: were the sources read correctly?

## What it found, and what was fixed

Each of these was a real wrong answer being taught, found by this audit and
fixed at source. They are listed because the pattern matters more than the
individual words: **a dictionary entry is not a meaning, and picking the wrong
part of one is how a learner ends up confidently wrong.**

| word | was taught as | is | why it went wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 拜拜 | "to die" | bye-bye | baai1baai3 and baai3baai3 are different words; a tone-only match grabbed the wrong one |
| 嚟 | "used in transliteration" | to come | the dictionary's first sense was a note about how the character is borrowed |
| 都 | "capital city" | all, also | a noun-only sense chosen for a word people use as a function word |
| 就 | "obey" | since…then | several senses packed in one string, and the first was the rarest |
| 搵 | "to wipe away tears" | to look for | a minority reading taken as the main one |
| 小豬 | "noun" | piglet | CC-Canto writes the part of speech as its own sense, and "noun" is short |
| 湯, 黃, 葉, 金 … | "Chinese surname" | soup, yellow, leaf, gold | every common character is also a surname, and dictionaries often print it first |
| 六 | nothing | six | no gloss in any source matched its Cantonese reading |

The fixes are rules, not patches: senses are split and tidied, a sense that
describes a usage is ranked below one that translates, a surname sense is
demoted, a bare part-of-speech is read as a label rather than a meaning, and —
the important one — **how well a reading fits now outranks how good the source
is**, so a fine Cantonese dictionary under the wrong reading cannot beat a
plain one under the right reading.

## Two new sources, because CC-Canto stops

words.hk is the right answer and needs a signed data request, which is yours to
make and will take a while. Until then:

- **English Wiktionary** (CC BY-SA 3.0), via kaikki.org's machine-readable
  extract — 86,198 Chinese words with a Jyutping reading and English senses,
  and, crucially, **senses tagged by variety**, so a Cantonese sense can be told
  from a Mandarin-only one. `build/wiktionary.mjs` boils 1.2 GB down to 8 MB.
- **Unihan** (Unicode licence) — Cantonese readings for 29,936 characters and
  English definitions for 20,587. It closed only five gaps, but it is the
  independent check on readings that the audit needed.

The effect on the top 1,000 spoken words:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| gloss matches the exact recorded reading | 625 | 905 |
| only a gloss for a different reading | 55 | 10 |
| no gloss at all | 27 | 17 |

## Where the meanings come from now

| words | share | source |
|---|---|---|
| 2,800 | 46.7% | CC-Canto — a Cantonese dictionary |
| 2,756 | 45.9% | Wiktionary |
| 216 | 3.6% | Wiktionary, sense marked Cantonese |
| 215 | 3.6% | CC-CEDICT — a Mandarin dictionary |
| 13 | 0.2% | Unihan |

**50.3%** of taught words have their meaning from a source that marks it as
Cantonese. The rest is shared Chinese vocabulary, which is legitimate — 水 is
water in both — but it is where a checker should look first.

## How sure the readings are

92.2% of taught words carry a gloss printed against that very Jyutping. The
rest are labelled in the app itself, on the word card: *sandhi* (2.6%, same word
in another tone), *inferred* (3.6%, no reading printed but only one exists),
*standard* (1.6%, taught under the standard reading rather than a recorded
variant).

Checked three ways — the corpus, rime-cantonese and Unihan — on the 1,305
single characters where all three speak: **88.0% agree exactly**, 11.6% agree on
the syllable and differ on the tone, and **5 genuinely differ** (啱, 零, 嘍, 押,
揞 — all cases where the corpus recorded a colloquial ng-dropping that the
dictionaries do not list).

## Ambiguity

574 English meanings are carried by more than one taught word — "only" is
carried by seven. **No question offers its own answer as a wrong answer**, which
is the fault that would make one unanswerable, and the build now checks that.
But it remains true that being asked for "outside" cannot distinguish 外 from
外面. That is a limit of testing a language through English, and the example
sentence on the answer card is the mitigation.

## What is left for a human

15 of 6,000 glosses (0.1%) are flagged as things a person should look at, and
**none of them is in the first 200 words**. The remainder are particles where a
description of usage genuinely is the meaning.

The real limit: I cannot hear Cantonese. Everything above is a check of sources
against each other. **The sample at `--sample 40` is meant for your wife**, in
small batches — it is the only test here that a speaker can run and I cannot.
