# User journey audit — 20 September 2026

Robert installed v1.0.0 on his phone and the first screen told him there was
nothing to do. This is what went wrong, in the order he met it.

## 1. The app said there was nothing to do, on a first run

The top line read **"Nothing due · 18 new"**. It was generated by a rule that
described the review queue — accurate, and written for someone who already had
a review queue. A learner who has never answered a question has nothing due by
definition, and the first thing the app said to him was the word *Nothing*.

**Fixed.** The line now depends on where you actually are: *"Ready when you
are"* on a first run, with *"Your first round — about five minutes"* under it
and a button that says **Start learning**. After that it says what is really
waiting: "12 to come back to, 5 new".

## 2. It spoke Cantonese at him, unasked

The word-of-the-day card carried a play button, and every play button
autoplayed 220ms after it rendered. That behaviour was written for listening
questions, where hearing it *is* the question, and it leaked to every screen.
So opening the app played a recorded sentence — 啲政客好識賺錢, "politicians are
good at raising money" — at a man who had not yet learnt the word for hello,
and left him wondering whether it meant he had a Cantonese voice installed. (He
does not. That was a human recording; the app had already told him, in smaller
type, that the voice was missing.)

**Fixed.** Autoplay is now opt-in per button and used in exactly three places,
all of them listening questions. Everywhere else, sound happens when you press.

## 3. The word of the day came from nowhere near where he was

It was drawn from the first 400 words of the whole list, so a learner on stage
one — hello, thank you, yes, no — was shown 賺 zaan6 "to earn", with an example
sentence about politicians. **Fixed:** it comes from the stage you are on.

## 4. Too much on one screen, in no particular order

Five blocks stacked with no hierarchy: today's count, a Start button, a stage
card, a warning about the missing voice, a word card, then seven menu rows.
Robert: *"it feels like I could select anything and head in the wrong
direction"*. That is what a flat page does — everything looks equally
important, so nothing is.

**Fixed.** One order, and it is an argument:

1. **What there is to do**, and the button that does it.
2. **Where that sits in the course** — stage, what it will let you do, distance to the gate.
3. **What this phone cannot do**, said once, quietly, with the detail folded away.
4. **Everything else**, reordered so the two things you would actually reach
   for are first: *The course*, then *Progress*, then the reference material.
5. **A word from this stage**, at the foot, where a thing to look at belongs.

## 5. The alarm that was not an alarm

*"6,145 questions need a Cantonese voice this phone does not have, and are set
aside"* — true, and it reads like a broken app. **Fixed:** the open line now
says what still works, and the count moved into the fold.

## 6. It did not feel light in the dark

The design was judged in light mode; his phone is dark. In dark the same
palette carried far more weight — a saturated coral slab for Start, headings a
size too large, and a near-black brown that flattened everything. **Fixed:**
softer accent, smaller headings, more air at the top, and a lighter
wrong-answer colour. Softening the accent brought it within ΔE 4.7 of the
verdict red under tritanopia, so the verdict colour moved rather than the
accent, and the colour audit passes again at 72 checks.

## 7. Things he asked for that were missing

- **Version codes and a notice when a new one is up.** The version was on the
  About page; now it is in Settings too, with a **What changed, and when** list,
  and the app watches for a new deploy and offers to reload.
- **Progress that cannot be lost.** It was saved, but only in this phone's
  browser storage — one "clear site data" from gone. Settings can now write a
  copy to a file and read one back.
- **Modules to track against.** The ten stages now appear on Progress with a
  mark against each, so "how far have I got" has a plain answer.

## What I would still watch

The home screen is calmer but it is still a scroll. If the menu rows turn out
to be rarely used, they belong behind one entry rather than seven.
